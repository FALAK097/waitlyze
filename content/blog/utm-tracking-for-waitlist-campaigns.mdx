If every post, email, partner, and advertisement points to the same plain waitlist URL, you can count signups but cannot explain where the useful ones came from. UTM parameters add a small attribution label to each link.

## The five UTM parameters

| Parameter | Answers | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `utm_source` | Who sent the traffic? | `linkedin`, `partner_acme` |
| `utm_medium` | What kind of channel? | `social`, `email`, `community` |
| `utm_campaign` | Which initiative? | `private_beta_nov` |
| `utm_content` | Which creative or placement? | `founder_post`, `footer_cta` |
| `utm_term` | Which paid keyword? | `waitlist_software` |

A tagged link might be:

```text
https://example.com/join?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=private_beta_nov&utm_content=founder_post
```

Use lowercase values, hyphens or underscores consistently, and no personal information. `linkedin`, `LinkedIn`, and `linkedin.com` become three rows in many analytics systems.

## Create a naming convention first

Maintain a small shared table with the approved values, owner, destination, and date. Keep source specific, medium reusable, campaign stable across channels, and content optional.

| Distribution | Source | Medium | Campaign | Content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Founder LinkedIn post | `linkedin` | `social` | `private_beta_nov` | `founder_story` |
| Subscriber update | `waitlist_email` | `email` | `private_beta_nov` | `progress_update_2` |
| Partner newsletter | `partner_acme` | `partner` | `private_beta_nov` | `newsletter_main` |

Do not encode the date in every field. The campaign name or your link registry can carry timing without making reports unreadable.

## Preserve referral and campaign context

A referral parameter answers “which subscriber shared?” while UTMs answer “where and in what campaign was the link distributed?” They can coexist:

```text
https://example.com/join?r=PERSONAL_CODE&utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=member_sharing
```

Waitlyze hosted and embedded signup flows preserve referral and UTM parameters. The analytics view groups acquisition by channel, referrer, campaign, and keyword. See [analytics](/docs/analytics) and [embed setup](/docs/embed).

## Measure quality after the click

Clicks are not the goal. Compare each source across the funnel:

1. visitors;
2. submitted signups;
3. confirmed subscribers;
4. marketing opt-ins where relevant;
5. completed referrals;
6. replies, beta participation, or launch activation in your downstream system.

A broad social post may create more signups while a niche partner creates fewer but more confirmations. The latter can be the better channel. Never treat UTM attribution as proof of causation: people often encounter several messages before using the final link.

## Common mistakes

- Tagging internal navigation overwrites the original acquisition context.
- Putting email addresses or names in UTM values leaks personal data into URLs and logs.
- Changing campaign spelling mid-launch fragments reporting.
- Using one campaign value for every initiative makes comparison impossible.
- Judging sources on signups without confirmation rewards low-quality traffic.
- Publishing tagged URLs as canonical URLs creates unnecessary duplicates.

## A pre-launch checklist

- Define source, medium, and campaign values.
- Generate and click every final link.
- Confirm parameters survive redirects and signup.
- Submit a test signup from each important path.
- Verify the source appears in analytics.
- Keep one clean canonical URL for search and sharing defaults.
- Review confirmed signups by source each week.

UTMs will not produce perfect multi-touch attribution. They will answer the practical early-stage question: which distribution choices are bringing people who care enough to complete the next step?
