A waitlist is easy to start and easy to neglect. The damage usually shows up on launch day, when a list that looked healthy produces almost no engagement. These are the mistakes behind that outcome.

## 1. Collecting emails and never sending any

This is the most common and the most damaging. People forget they signed up within a few weeks. When your launch email arrives three months later, it reads like spam.

**Fix:** plan a simple rhythm before you publish. A welcome email right after confirmation, a progress update every two to four weeks, a countdown shortly before launch, and a launch-day email. Put the dates on a calendar.

## 2. Asking for too much at signup

Every extra field costs signups. Name, company, role, phone number, use case: each one is a reason to leave. You can ask for all of it later, by email, from people who already want to hear from you.

**Fix:** one email field and one button. If you need segmentation, ask in a follow-up email with a one-click reply.

## 3. No confirmation step

Without email confirmation you end up with typos, bots, and addresses that bounce. Your sending reputation suffers before you have sent a single real campaign.

**Fix:** require confirmation before a signup counts. Confirmed subscribers are the ones worth counting, and they are the only ones who should earn referral credit or queue position.

## 4. Measuring only the total

A big list of people who never open your email is not an audience. The total hides the real problem until launch day.

**Fix:** track conversion rate, confirmation rate, sources, and email engagement alongside the total. A small list of confirmed, engaged people is worth more than a large cold one.

## 5. Launching to everyone at once

Opening the door to the entire list on day one means a bug or a confusing onboarding step hits your most excited users first, with no chance to fix it.

**Fix:** invite the most engaged slice first, fix what breaks, then widen. Thank people for waiting and tell them what you learned. That closes the loop.

## 6. Fake scarcity

"Only 10 spots left" when there are not 10 spots left trains people to ignore you and erodes trust at the moment you need it most.

**Fix:** use real constraints. A real launch date, a real founding-member window, or a real capacity limit. If there is no real constraint, do not invent one.

## 7. A page that only works on desktop

Most visitors arrive on a phone. A form that is hard to tap or a button that sits below the fold on mobile quietly halves your conversion.

**Fix:** preview both layouts before publishing and test the form on an actual phone.

## 8. No sharing preview

When someone shares your link, the preview card is a mini landing page. A blank or ugly preview wastes the share.

**Fix:** set a clear title, description, and image so the link looks good in chat apps and social feeds.

## 9. Treating the thank-you page as an ending

The moment after signup is the highest intent you will get from a subscriber. A dead "thanks, we'll be in touch" page wastes it.

**Fix:** show position, a personal referral link, and a one-tap share button. Turn the join into a sharing moment.

## 10. Never cleaning the list

Over time, inactive subscribers drag down your deliverability and your metrics. A list you never prune becomes a liability.

**Fix:** periodically remove or re-engage subscribers who never confirm or never open. A smaller, healthier list sends better.

## The short version

Keep the form short, confirm every signup, email on a schedule, measure more than the total, and treat the thank-you page as a product. Those five habits separate a list that launches well from one that does not.
