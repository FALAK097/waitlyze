Your waitlist page has a single job: get the right visitor to type their email address. Use this checklist before you publish, and again whenever conversion drops.

## Above the fold

### 1. A headline that states the outcome

Say what the visitor gets, in plain language. "Never miss a bill again" is clearer than "The future of personal finance."

### 2. One supporting sentence

Explain who it is for and how it works, in one line. If a stranger cannot repeat it back, simplify.

### 3. A single email field and a specific button

One field. A button that says what happens, such as "Join the waitlist" or "Get early access", rather than "Submit."

### 4. A reason to join now

Visitors need a nudge. Examples: early access, a founding-member offer, limited spots, or a launch date. Make it truthful, because fake scarcity damages trust and gets noticed.

### 5. Something to look at

A product screenshot, a short demo, or a mockup helps visitors picture the result. If you do not have the product yet, a clear illustration of the outcome works.

## Below the fold

### 6. Proof

Choose the proof you honestly have:

- A count of people already on the list, if it is meaningful.
- A short quote from a tester or advisor.
- Logos or names of known people who have agreed to be mentioned.
- A one-line origin story that shows why you are qualified to build this.

### 7. What happens next

Tell people exactly what to expect after signing up: a confirmation email, then a welcome message, then updates, then access. Uncertainty makes people hesitate.

### 8. A short FAQ

Answer the questions that block signups: when it launches, what it costs if you know, who it is for, what you do with their email, and how to unsubscribe.

### 9. A second signup form

On a long page, repeat the form at the bottom so a convinced visitor does not need to scroll up.

## Trust and polish

### 10. A privacy note

A single line near the form, such as "We only email you about launch updates. Unsubscribe any time," reduces hesitation.

### 11. Speed and mobile

Most visitors arrive on a phone. Check that the page loads quickly, the form is easy to tap, the button is visible without scrolling, and nothing overflows the screen. Preview both layouts before publishing.

### 12. A sharing preview

When someone shares your link, the preview card is a mini landing page. Set a clear title, description, and image so the share looks good in chat apps and social feeds.

## Pick a layout that fits your launch

The right page structure depends on what you are launching. A developer tool benefits from a docs-first layout with workflow proof, a restaurant opening from a seasonal page with tasting details, and a newsletter from an editorial page with reader proof. ## After you publish

A landing page is never finished.

1. **Watch conversion.** If many people visit and few sign up, the promise or the proof needs work.
2. **Test one change at a time.** Change the headline, then wait for enough visits to see an effect before changing anything else.
3. **Check where visitors come from.** A page can convert well for one source and poorly for another, which tells you where to promote it.

## The checklist, condensed

- [ ] Headline states the outcome
- [ ] One supporting sentence
- [ ] One field and a specific button
- [ ] A truthful reason to join now
- [ ] A visual of the product or result
- [ ] Honest proof
- [ ] What happens next
- [ ] Short FAQ
- [ ] Second form at the bottom
- [ ] Privacy note
- [ ] Fast and mobile-friendly
- [ ] Good sharing preview

If you can tick every box, you are ready to publish. If not, fix the unchecked ones first. Each is cheaper to improve before launch than after.
