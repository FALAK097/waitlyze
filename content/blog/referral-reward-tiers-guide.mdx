A referral reward gives subscribers a reason to share. The difficult part is choosing an incentive that attracts future users, feels achievable, and remains affordable if the campaign grows.

## Start with the behavior

For most waitlists, the desired behavior is: invite people who genuinely want the product and complete signup confirmation. That excludes rewards for views, clicks, or raw submissions, which are easy to inflate.

Choose one campaign goal: reach qualified peers, identify engaged testers, fill a beta in priority order, or reduce founder-led promotion. The goal determines the reward. If you need testers, early access is aligned. If you need feedback, a group product session may work better than merchandise.

## Score every reward idea

| Quality | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Relevant | Would someone who does not want the product chase it? |
| Valuable | Will the intended subscriber share for it? |
| Scalable | Can you deliver it if ten times more people qualify? |
| Clear | Can you explain the benefit in one sentence? |
| Verifiable | Can you objectively decide when it is earned? |

Product rewards include earlier access, trial extensions, credits, extra usage, and priority onboarding. Access rewards include a private community, group walkthrough, roadmap vote, or founding-member recognition. Digital templates and workshops can scale; one-to-one services should be capped.

Cash, gift cards, and physical products add fraud, shipping, tax, geography, and support considerations. Get appropriate legal and tax advice for high-value campaigns or competitions.

## Build a simple ladder

Use an achievable first win, a valuable core reward, and a scarce advocate tier.

| Verified referrals | Example reward | Reason |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 3 | Founding badge and template pack | Low cost and attainable |
| 5 | Priority beta access | Directly tied to product interest |
| 10 | Three months of a paid plan | Valuable and bounded |
| 25 | Group setup session | Scarce benefit for advocates |

These are examples, not benchmarks. Base thresholds on how many relevant people a subscriber can realistically reach, then adjust future campaigns from observed behavior.

## Model the cost

`Expected reward cost = expected qualifiers × fulfillment cost per reward`

Include staff review, support time, shipping, vendor fees, scarce capacity, and disputes. Run a stress case at two or three times the expected number of qualifiers. If it would force you to change the rules, redesign the offer before launch.

## Write eligibility rules plainly

State what counts, whether confirmation is required, how duplicates and self-referrals are treated, when progress updates, whether suspicious activity can be reviewed, how rewards arrive, and any limits.

Waitlyze credits eligible referrals after the invited person verifies and the referral is clear or approved. The [referral guide](/blog/referral-waitlist-guide) explains the flow and fraud controls.

## Separate unlocking from fulfillment

Reaching a tier and delivering the benefit are different operations. For valuable rewards:

1. record the eligible referral event;
2. deduplicate it with a stable event ID;
3. review suspicious patterns;
4. fulfill it in your product or operations system;
5. record delivery so it cannot happen twice.

Waitlyze exposes `referral.completed` and reward-tier milestones through signed webhooks. Use the verified event—not a signup alone—to drive fulfillment. See the [webhook documentation](/docs/webhooks).

## Avoid these mistakes

- A prize unrelated to the product grows the wrong audience.
- Too many tiers make the program hard to explain.
- Unbounded “lifetime” promises create hidden long-term costs.
- Rewarding submissions invites throwaway accounts.
- Hidden rules create disputes with the people you want as advocates.

Before publishing, write down the intended invitee, eligibility event, maximum stress-case cost, fulfillment owner, delivery time, review signals, and public rules link. If any answer is vague, the campaign is not ready.

The right ladder gives future users more of what they already value—and gives you growth you can afford to honor.
