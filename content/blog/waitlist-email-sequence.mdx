A waitlist without emails is just a list. The messages you send between signup and launch decide whether people are excited, indifferent, or have forgotten you by the time you open the doors.

Here is a simple sequence that works for most launches.

## Before you send anything

Three rules keep your list healthy:

1. **Email confirmed subscribers only.** Confirmation proves the address is real and belongs to the person who joined.
2. **Respect consent.** Confirming an address is not the same as agreeing to marketing emails. Send marketing messages only to people who opted in, and honor unsubscribes immediately.
3. **Send from a person or a name people recognize.** "Priya from Acme" gets opened more than "no-reply."

## The sequence at a glance

| When | Email | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Right after confirmation | Welcome | Set expectations and give a reason to stay |
| 2 to 4 days later | Referral reminder (optional) | Prompt sharing while excitement is high |
| Every 2 to 4 weeks | Progress update | Stay present and build trust |
| When milestones are hit | Milestone note | Celebrate the list and invite more sharing |
| 3 to 7 days before launch | Countdown | Create anticipation |
| Launch day | Launch | Drive action |
| 2 to 3 days after launch | Last chance or thank you | Catch the people who missed it |

## 1. The welcome email

This is your most-read email, so use it well.

- **Subject ideas:** "You're on the list", "Welcome to the [product] waitlist", "Here's what happens next".
- **Say:** thank them, repeat the promise, explain what they will get and when, and tell them how to reply.
- **Do:** ask one easy question, such as "What would you use this for?" Replies help you learn and also improve deliverability.
- **Skip:** long backstories and multiple links.

## 2. The referral reminder

If you run a referral loop, send a short nudge a few days after joining to anyone who has not shared yet. Remind them of the reward, give them their personal link, and include a ready-to-send sentence they can paste. Read more in [how a referral waitlist works](/blog/referral-waitlist-guide).

## 3. Progress updates

Updates are what keep a list warm. Each one should contain one useful thing:

- A behind-the-scenes look at what you built.
- A decision you made and why.
- A request for feedback on a specific choice.
- A small preview, such as a screenshot or short clip.

Keep them short, write like a person, and be honest about delays. Admitting a slip builds more trust than silence.

**Subject ideas:** "What we shipped this month", "Quick question about [feature]", "A first look".

## 4. Milestone emails

When the list reaches a number worth celebrating, tell people. It gives everyone a reason to open the email, thanks them for helping, and gives you a natural moment to ask them to share. Waitlyze offers welcome, referral reminder, and milestone automations so you can set these up once instead of sending each by hand. See [automations](/docs/automations).

## 5. The countdown

A few days before launch, tell people exactly when it happens and what to do. Give the date and time with a time zone, say whether access opens in waves, and link to anything they should prepare.

**Subject ideas:** "We open on Thursday", "3 days to go", "Your invite is almost ready".

## 6. The launch email

Keep this one simple. A single clear call to action, a one-line reminder of the benefit, and a link that works. If access opens in waves, say which wave the reader is in.

## 7. The follow-up

Send a short message two or three days later to people who did not click. A different angle, a testimonial from an early user, or a simple "the door is still open" works better than repeating the first email.

## Make each email easy to act on

- One goal per email. If you ask for three things, you get none.
- Write the subject line last, and make it specific. Avoid all caps, excess punctuation, and vague teasers.
- Preview on mobile. Most people read on a phone.
- Include a plain unsubscribe link. It protects your reputation more than it costs you subscribers.

## Check what works

Look at opens, clicks, replies, and unsubscribes after each send. A rising unsubscribe rate means your emails are too frequent or off-topic. A steady stream of replies means you are building a relationship. For more on reading these numbers, see [waitlist metrics that actually matter](/blog/waitlist-metrics-that-matter).
